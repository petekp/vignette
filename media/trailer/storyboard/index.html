The film says one thing

Your agent can show you things, and you answer by pointing.

It is built around one moment: Claude's options arrive as a card in Vignette, and you answer by drawing on them.

  1. You capture the itinerary with macOS's own shortcut. The card lands in the corner.
  2. You click the card and draw what you want: an arrow from a stop on the map to the day it belongs to, "number the days", and a box around the Oct 14 day, "make this pop, options?"
  3. You send it to Claude Code.
  4. Claude shows you something back: here, three versions of the card, labelled A, B and C.
  5. You point: a box around C, "this one, with that flag", and an arrow from B's Highlight flag.
  6. Claude builds it. The day numbers went in on its first turn, since they had one right answer.

Criteria, applied on every pass

  1. Every beat serves the sentence, or it is cut.
  2. The screen proves each caption. Watch the animatic with captions off (C).
  3. A first-time viewer follows it at real speed.
  4. Nothing is in the film only because a feature needs showing. That is what the loops are for.
  5. Cut before adding.

How to read a panel

Each panel is the exact 16:10 crop the viewer sees at the shot's marked time, with its caption. The desktop, window positions, card slots and editor frame are measured from the last take. The Postcard pages are real renders. The small map beside each panel shows where the camera is on the whole screen, in red, and where it came from, dashed.

Anything marked approximate is decided by the app or by Claude during the take. Vignette's own UI is redrawn here, not captured.

Rules for every shot

  • Camera. A move starts only after what it leaves has been still, and settles over 1.6 to 2.0 s on a spring. It never moves while the thing on screen is changing.
  • Wide for handoffs. Any moment where two windows matter is WIDE: a card landing, Send, Reply, a card from Claude arriving.
  • Captions. A dark pill, top left, 34 px at 1600 wide, with key caps. A key cap lights for 0.45 s when its key is pressed. A new caption fades in over 0.25 s.
  • Cuts. Claude's working time is always inside a dissolve of 0.5 s, which starts and ends with the pointer still.
  • Pointer. It glides, and pauses 0.3 to 0.6 s before each press.
