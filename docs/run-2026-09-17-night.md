## 1. What landed

### The room gives way once, when a zoom is aimed
`todo5/zoom`, `e041dc2`.

There is no arc in the zoom's code. What you saw is the frame's clamp against its room.
`Zoom.frame(within:)` honoured the anchor exactly until one edge of the frame reached the room, and
took it back on every tick after that: the picture held still while it grew, then slid, and a point
that had been moving one way turned around.

Two new pieces of geometry replace that. `Zoom.reach(fitted:within:)` is how far the window can grow
before it fills the room, and `AnnotationController.maxZoom` is now that function, so the concept has
one definition. `Zoom.anchor(_:fitting:within:)` moves the anchor as little as the room allows, so
the window can grow all the way to the reach without the frame ever touching the room's edge. Each
edge of the frame moves one way as the window grows, so an anchor that fits the whole growth fits
every scale on the way to it: the frame's path is one straight line and the clamp never bites. The
clamp in `Zoom.frame` stays as the last guard.

`Zoom.aim` also starts a step from that anchor rather than from one read at the fitted size. At scale
1 the frame is the fitted frame whatever the anchor, so there is no growth for an anchor to describe;
blending from that made-up value bowed the path.

The agent's numbers, on a 1000x700 point grid fixture with the stack closed, from a tick log at
120 Hz and a 60 fps recording read back frame by frame:

| Cmd+plus from rest | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the anchor over the step | 0.5 for 110 ms, then given away | 0.3552 throughout, one value |
| first movement of the picture | none for 110 ms | 0.84 pt on the first tick |
| biggest movement in one refresh | 2.54 pt, out of a standstill | 1.68 pt, decaying with the spring |
| the line a quarter down the image | up 31.6 pt, then back down 9.7 pt | up 18.0 pt, and stops |

A 24-message cmd+wheel stream: the biggest movement in one refresh fell from 4.88 pt to 1.06 pt, and
the anchor is 0.3552 from the first tick instead of walking 0.221 → 0.354 over 60 ms. Offline, over a
walk of 240 scales, the clamp moves the frame by 0.000 pt after the change (21.1 pt before).

Verified in my round with the narrowed room, which the agent could not reach: section 2 has it.

Not verified by either of us: a real trackpad pinch, a real two-finger double tap, a second display.

### A zoom holds the edge of the picture its cursor is near
`todo5/zoom`, `a6f64f8`.

`Zoom.pulledToEdges(_:band:pull:)` moves the cursor onto the nearest edge of the picture before
`aimPan` stores it. Two tweaks, both with bounds and sliders in the tweak panel's Annotator section:

- `ui.zoomEdgeBand` = 0.15, bounds 0…0.5: how far from each edge the pull reaches, as a fraction of
  the picture. On a 986 point frame that is 148 points.
- `ui.zoomEdgePull` = 0.5, bounds 0…1: the part of that band in which the edge is taken outright.
  Across the rest the pull eases off linearly to nothing at the band's inner edge.

Why it was needed: the magnification holds the image's point under the cursor at the cursor's own
fraction of the window. With the cursor at 0.95 the visible part runs to `0.95 + 0.05 / camera`,
which is short of the image's right edge at every magnification above 1. Only a cursor at exactly 1
keeps the edge. "Within a few pixels" was exact.

The pull is in the magnification alone. Until the window can grow no further the camera is exactly 1
and the whole image is inside the window, so nothing can be cropped there. `Zoom.split` is untouched.

The agent's counterfactual was the same build with `ui.zoomEdgeBand` set to 0, which is the old
behaviour exactly. At `zoomLevel 3.320` / `canvasZoom 2.623` on a 1000x700 px image:

| cursor | `zoomEdgeBand` | `page.visible` | the corner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.95 / 0.95 | 0 | `[588, 412, 382, 267]` | 30 px right and 21 px below the view |
| 0.95 / 0.95 | 0.15 | `[618, 433, 382, 267]` | exactly at the view's corner |
| 0.05 / 0.05 | 0.15 | `[0, 0, 515, 360]` | the top left corner exactly at the view's corner |
| 0.50 / 0.50 | 0.15 | `[309, 217, 382, 267]` | centred; the middle zooms about itself |

Verified again in my round, on a different fixture and with the stack showing: section 2.

## 2. Decisions made without you, and open questions

### Zoom

- **The room is divided at the full growth, not at each step's target.** The agent built the per-step
  version first and measured it: a cmd+wheel stream's target is only one message ahead, so the
  give-way still arrived when the room ran out — the same jerk, about 100 ms softer, with the peak
  down from 4.9 to 3.8 points a refresh. Clamping at the reach settles the anchor on the first tick
  of a gesture and it never moves again during it.
- **The cost, stated plainly.** In the direction the room binds there is exactly one anchor, so the
  cursor's point is not held there; the picture pans at a steady rate as it grows. On the fixture
  that is 0.9 points for a 1 per cent step, and the total displacement over a whole zoom is
  unchanged. **One thing to judge**: if the steady pan reads as drift rather than smoothness, the
  alternative is to let the window stop growing earlier so the cursor is held exactly. That costs
  screen area and makes the window's scale depend on where the cursor was, which `Zoom.split`
  deliberately does not.
- **Inside the edge band the zoom no longer holds the point under the cursor. It holds the edge.**
  That is the whole of the change. **One thing to judge**: 0.15 of the picture is generous. If the
  outer sixth feels sticky, drop `ui.zoomEdgeBand` toward 0.08. If corners still escape when you
  meant to keep them, raise `ui.zoomEdgePull` toward 1, which pins anywhere inside the band.
- **No new tweak for the anchor change.** It is a geometric correction, not a number to tune.

### What was found and fixed

**A zoom-out shrank the window off-centre and slid the picture.** `Zoom.aim` passed every target
through the room's anchor, including a target below 1 — the short pull a zoom-out makes before it
springs back. Below the fitted size there is no growth for the room to divide: a shrunk frame is
inside the fitted one, which the room contains. Clamping there only moved the shrink's centre. The
reviewer's arithmetic on the zoom agent's own fixture: the picture's middle drifted 4.99 pt at one
Cmd+minus, 8.98 pt at level 0.7, and 14.97 pt at the deepest pull, and back on the spring home.
That is the motion item 22 removed, in the other direction, on a path nobody had measured.

The fix takes the room's anchor only when the step grows. Driven, on the fixture the zoom agent
used — fitted `[263, 103, 986, 689]` in a room `[0, 38, 1512, 872]`, 65 points above and 118 below:

| step | `zoomAnchor` | the frame, measured in from the fitted one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cmd+plus | `[0.5, 0.3552]` | grows into the whole room: `[132, 38, 1248, 872]` |
| Cmd+0 | `[0.5, 0.3552]` | home at `[263, 103, 986, 689]` |
| Cmd+minus, eight probes through the pull | `[0.5, 0.5]` at every one | top 5, 17, 25, 30, 34, 35, 36, 37 against bottom 5, 18, 25, 30, 33, 36, 36, 36 |

So the growing step still takes the room's one allowed anchor, 0.3552, exactly the agent's
published number; the shrinking step takes the middle, and the top and the bottom come in by the
same amount at every probe, within the frame's rounding to whole points. Before the fix the anchor
through that pull would have been 0.3552 and the top would have taken 71% of the shrink. The pull
sprang back to exactly `[263, 103, 986, 689]`.
