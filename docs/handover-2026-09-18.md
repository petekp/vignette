# Drawing the moment the card lands (2026-09-18)

Pete: "see what we can do to decrease the time between the thumbnail -> annotator transition coming
to a rest and being able to draw/manipulate the image; there's a noticeable delay here possibly due
to our easing transition and gating interaction."

This note measures that delay on the unchanged build, says what the two gates really were, and
measures the same driven sequence after the change.

## The driven sequence

One fixture (`screencapture -x -R 200,200,900,560`) in the watch folder, the recent stack open with
that one card at `[1307, 773, 188, 120]`, and `vignette://annotate?file=…`. The annotator's frame is
`[126, 98, 1125, 699]`, so the card's left edge travels 1181 points.

Recorded with `screencapture -x -v -R 60,300,760,560`, which holds the annotator frame's left edge
and its toolbar. Per frame: the sub-pixel column of the strongest left-to-right brightness rise,
which is the picture's left edge (it rests at capture column 131.0), and the mean brightness of a
box inside the toolbar, which jumps when the toolbar appears. Times below are given against the
frame at which the flight snaps onto its target, which is `arrived` and is unmistakable in the
trace.

## The two gates, before

**`show`, at `Anim.settle`.** `perform(.prepare)` gave `fly` one callback, `arrived`, at
`Anim.settle(expandDuration, bounce: 0.15, distance: travel, within: 0.5)` — 0.604 s for this
flight. That sent `shown`, and `.show` put the window up, made it key, activated the app, started
the outside-click monitor and showed the toolbar. Three runs: `[transition] annotate` to
`[transition] shown` was **642, 642 and 644 ms**.

**The lift, at `[annotate] loaded`.** `.show` lifted the flight image only if the page had already
reported the key; otherwise `pageLoaded` did. Three runs: **692, 693 and 696 ms** after `load` was
sent, which is 50, 51 and 52 ms after `show`.

That second gate cannot be moved by sending `load` earlier, and the reason matters. `load` is
already sent in `prepare`, and the page puts the image on the canvas synchronously; only the
`loaded` report waits, because it is posted from a double `requestAnimationFrame` and WebKit pauses
frames for a hidden window. Driven directly, with the annotator hidden:

```
[eval] ok NO raf within 1500ms, hidden=true
```

So `loaded` could never arrive before the window came up. The two gates were one gate: `show`.

**Where the eye sees the card stop.** From the recordings, the last frame whose edge moves by more
than one capture pixel (half a point) is **233, 233 and 267 ms before `arrived`**. After that the
card creeps back through the last of its overshoot at under half a point a frame. So the window
came up about a quarter of a second after the motion was over.

**What a press in that quarter second did.** A real CGEvent drag was posted inside the annotator's
frame at a measured instant (`captures/timed-drag.swift`). With the mouse-down 604 ms and 639 ms
after `[transition] annotate` — both a few milliseconds before `show` would have fired — the page
got nothing and the click reached the stack's own outside-click monitor:

```
[transition] annotate(Screenshot t1.png from stack) -> flyingOut(…) effects=prepare(…)
[transition] dismiss -> parking(Screenshot t1.png then dismiss) effects=park(…)
[transition] parked -> idle effects=hideAnnotator
```

`page.shapes` 0, `annotator.windowVisible` false: the session was gone. With the mouse-down at
689 ms, 49 ms after `show`, `page.shapes` went to **2** — the image and a rectangle. So the delay
was not only a wait; a drag started during it threw the session away.

## What changed

`fly` now answers twice. The second answer, `arrived`, is unchanged and still owns the picture: the
flight is put exactly on the target, the annotator takes the shadow back, and the flight image is
lifted. The first, `covered`, runs at `Anim.passesTarget` — the moment the spring first reaches its
target.

That moment is what makes the window safe to put up. A spring with bounce goes past its target and
comes back, so from there until it settles the flight's rect contains the target on every side, and
a window drawn at the target is behind the picture. The one thing that would show is a shadow,
because a shadow falls outside the frame it is cast from; so `AnnotationController.show` puts the
window up with `shadowOpacity` 0 and `landed()` turns it on at `arrived`, in the same run loop turn
the flight drops its own. A spring with no bounce never reaches its target, and `fly` takes the
earlier of `passesTarget` and `settle`, so no overshoot means no early cover.

Everything that makes the editor usable moved to `show`: the key window, the app's activation, the
outside-click monitor and the toolbar. Nothing visible moved except the toolbar, which now appears
as the card finishes its path instead of a third of a second later.

A flight can also go without arriving, and then the window would keep a shadow that is switched
off. `fly` takes a third callback, `dropped`, which `end(id:)` and `endAll()` run for a flight that
had not arrived, and the annotator takes its shadow back there too. The path that reaches it: a
lone thumbnail's panel empties when the annotator opens, so a screenshot arriving in the next
third of a second presents the panel anew, and `present` takes the whole layer down. Driven — a
`cp` into the watch folder 350 ms after `vignette://annotate`, which gave `[transition] newShot(…)
-> annotating(…) effects=join(…)` 51 ms after `show` — the band outside the frame's left edge reads
15.40 15.39 15.31 15.25 15.08 14.92 … 14.31 and then the ring at 118.00, the same numbers to two
decimals as a session with nothing racing it.

The reducer is untouched. `shown` is simply sent at the earlier moment, so `annotating` now means
"the window is up", which it always meant; what it no longer means is "the flight is over".

### Why not the other candidates

- **Send `load` earlier.** It is already sent in `prepare`, and the measurement above shows the
  page's report is gated on the window, not on the decode.
- **Put the window up early at the exact target and lift the flight with it.** That is the step
  `docs/shadow-2026-09-17.md` measured at three points in one frame, and it is what this design
  avoids by leaving the picture with the flight.
- **Tighten the spring's tail** (`bounce`, or `TransitionLayer.arrivalTolerance`). `bounce` is the
  shape of the landing, and the tolerance buys little: from the model, 0.5 points to 2 points is
  75 ms and to 4 points is 125 ms, against the 312 ms the cover moment gives, and every point of it
  is a point the picture steps.

## The two gates, after

Same sequence, same region, three runs each. The control is the same worktree with the change
stashed, built and driven in the same session, so the machine is the same.

| | `annotate` → `show` | `annotate` → `loaded` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before | 642, 642, 644 ms | 692, 693, 696 ms |
| after | 337, 327, 330 ms | 370, 371, 374 ms |

`show` is **312 ms earlier**. `loaded` now arrives about 250 ms before `arrived` instead of 50 ms
after it, so the flight image is lifted in the same turn the flight lands rather than three frames
later.

**A drag lands as soon as the card has travelled its path.** Mouse-down measured against
`[transition] annotate`:

| mouse-down | `show` | result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +302 ms | +330 ms | the stack was dismissed, `page.shapes` 0 |
| +342 ms | +327 ms | **`page.shapes` 2** |
| +381 ms | +330 ms | **`page.shapes` 2** |
| +444 ms | +331 ms | **`page.shapes` 2** |

The card first reaches the annotator's frame at about +330 ms, which is the same moment. So a
rectangle dragged as the card arrives lands on the page.

**The first few milliseconds after the window appears are still a race**, which is not new and is
not the spring. `OutsideClick` asks the window server which window is under the cursor; for a
window ordered in a moment ago the answer can still be the window behind it, and the click is then
read as outside, which ends the session and closes the stack. One press of six, 18 ms after `show`,
did that:

```
22:28:35.534 [transition] shown -> annotating(Screenshot t1.png) effects=show
22:28:35.556 [annotate] cancelled
22:28:35.556 [transition] close -> parking(Screenshot t1.png then close) effects=park(…)
```

The other five, 15, 23, 42, 67 and 109 ms after `show`, all drew. Before the change the same race
was there at the old `show`, a third of a second later, where nobody was pressing yet.

**The picture and the shadow still do not step**, on the shadow doc's own method. The band 0 to 14
points outside the frame's left edge, across the handover, biggest change from one frame to the
next:

| run | band across the handover | biggest step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| before | 18.56 18.53 18.56 18.54 18.57 18.56 18.56 18.56 18.57 18.58 | 0.03 |
| after | 18.43 18.46 18.48 18.57 18.54 18.56 18.56 18.56 18.56 18.58 | 0.28 |

The flicker the shadow doc fixed was 1.85 grey levels in one frame. Neither of these is a
handover: the small movement in the "after" column is the flight's own edge crossing the band as it
settles.

**One thing got slightly worse.** The picture's step at `arrived` — the flight being put exactly on
the target after the spring's last fraction — grew from 0.9 capture pixels (0.45 points) to 1.7
(0.85 points), over three runs each. With the annotator's window up, the flight's last frames are
rendered a little further from the target at the same wall time; the window server has a web view
to paint that it did not have before. It is still under a point, and moving the cover moment 165 ms
earlier did not reduce it, so it is not a burst of work at the handover. If it ever shows, the fix
is to let `arrived` wait for the spring's own settling time — 113 ms later, and nothing the user
waits for is behind it any more.

**`ui.motion: 0`.** `expandDuration` is 0, so `passesTarget` and `settle` are both 0 and the two
callbacks run in that order in the same turn: `annotate -> flyingOut` and `shown -> annotating` 9 ms
apart, `loaded` at 47 ms, the whole handover at once.

**The card's return is untouched.** `returnCard` passes no `covered` callback, so its only answer is
`arrived`, as before. Driven and recorded over the card's slot, the band 1 to 12 points below the
card decays smoothly as the card lands — 30.4, 30.0, 29.0, 27.5, 25.9, 24.5, 24.6, 24.5, 24.3 — and
settles at 24.89 with no frame changing by more than 0.1.
