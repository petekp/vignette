# Live ink: what would make it ten times more useful (2026-10-05)

Status: superseded by `docs/live-ink-gestures-2026-10-05.md`. Nothing here is built.

## The answer

Today live ink is a conversation. You ask one thing, then you watch until it is done. Each ask
holds your full attention for about 25 to 30 s, and the next ask waits for the last one.

Ten times more useful means a review pass. You go through your app and mark ten things in a minute,
without waiting. Each ask runs on its own. Vignette checks each result against the live window, and
each mark turns green where it was drawn. You look only at the marks that did not.

That cuts your attention per ask from about 30 s to about 5 s, and asks no longer queue behind you.
It needs four changes, in this order:

1. Asks that do not wait for each other.
2. A check Vignette makes after each ask, so you do not have to.
3. The element under the ink, sent with the ask.
4. Marks that survive leaving the page.

Voice for the note would help every step, but none of them depends on it.

## What the takes measured

The stage's Claude Code answered 91 live ink asks on 2026-10-05. Their transcripts are in
`~/Code/vignette/media/trailer/out/stage/claude/projects/-Users-petepetrash-Code-postcard/`.

| Ask | Asks | Session time, median |
|---|---|---|
| "number the days like the map" | 27 | 13.7 s |
| "stack these on mobile" | 23 | 6.5 s |
| "what else should we fix on mobile?" | 21 | 16.4 s |
| "Fix both", a clicked action | 19 | 9.4 s |
| All | 91 | 12.4 s |

The session's time split like this:

| Step | Share |
|---|---|
| Writing edits | 37% |
| Composing a drawn answer | 20% |
| Writing the closing message | 20% |
| Reading the ask | 15% |
| Finding the code | 8% |
| Checking the result | 0% |

Four findings decide the direction:

- **You are the bottleneck.** In take `take-20261005-185039`, drawing and typing took about 11 s
  per ask, and waiting took 7 to 16 s. Vignette takes one ask at a time. A new ask clears the last
  answer (`LiveInk.ask` calls `removeAnswer()`), and one `working` slot watches one session.
- **Nobody checks the result but you.** One ask in 91 ran anything after its edit, and that was a
  grep. The agent's replies say it has not looked. The loop is fast because you watch the page
  reload. The screenshot asks of late September did check, and spent 7 to 96 s of 30 to 216 s
  doing it.
- **The ask names no element.** It carries the note, the app, the window title, the URL and a
  picture with the ink burned in. The session matched visible words to source by string, and
  grepped for selectors in 6 asks.
- **The demo hides the cost of finding code.** Postcard has four files, and every take first asks
  which files make up the page. Finding code took 8% here. A real repository would take more.

A faster agent alone gives no more than about twice the speed. The session's 12 s could perhaps
become 6 s, but you still draw, wait and look, one ask at a time.

## The four changes

### 1. Asks that do not wait

Each ask owns its marks, its request and its state, from sent to resolved. A new ask leaves older
answers where they are. Vignette already keeps sent asks by request id (`sending`). The single
`asking` and `working` state becomes one state per ask.

A Claude Code session takes the monitor's events between turns, so asks sent to one session run in
order without anything new. Sending them to several sessions at once is a later choice.

You see each ask's state on its own ink: sent, working, done, or needs you. A small list of the
pass's asks, by page, shows the ones out of view.

### 2. Vignette checks each result

When the session's turn ends, Vignette captures the window again, around the ink. It sends the
before and after pictures and your note to the responder, which answers in about 2 s with no tools.
It answers one question: does the after picture do what the note asked?

- **Yes:** the ink turns green and fades, as it does today.
- **No or unsure:** the ink stays, with one line on why. That is the mark you look at.

Doing this outside the session costs about 2 s, not the 7 to 96 s the session spent with
screenshots, and it costs no session tokens. The ask still has your note as the standard to check
against.

### 3. The element under the ink

The packet adds what Accessibility says is under the ink: the element's role, its label, its
rectangle on screen, and in a browser its `AXDOMIdentifier` and class list. On a local dev server, a
small script on the page could add the source file and line, as React Grab does.

This helps three places at once:

- **The session** starts at the element, not at a grep. This matters most in a large repository.
- **The check** compares that element before and after, not a guessed region.
- **Drawn answers** name rectangles. Today the agent guesses fractions of the image, such as
  `"box": [0.06, 0.615, 0.2, 0.03]`, which is most of its 10 s of composing.

### 4. Marks that survive leaving the page

A review pass moves between pages. Today a mark belongs to its window and goes when the window
closes. For a pass, a mark is also kept by URL, with the words under it as its anchor, as
Hypothesis does. It comes back when the page comes back. A mark whose words are gone goes to the
list rather than vanishing.

## The first experiment

Before building any of this, test whether the review pass holds up.

- **Question:** with five asks drawn in a row on Postcard, sent to one session without waiting,
  does the checker in change 2 agree with what a person sees?
- **What would change the decision:** if the checker is wrong on more than one ask in five, change
  2 needs the element from change 3 first, and the order above swaps.
- **Budget:** a script on the stage, using `live-ink-ask` and the existing captures, and one take.
  The take uses the Mac for about 3 minutes, so it needs Pete's go-ahead.

## Left out on purpose

- **A faster agent.** It helps, but by about twice at most, as above.
- **More gestures and handwriting.** They make each ask nicer. They do not change how many asks a
  person can make.
- **Sharing ink with other people.** It is worth doing once a pass works for one person.
