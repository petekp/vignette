# Notes on tags (2026-09-29)

A note becomes a rounded tag filled with its mark colour, and the black outline around its letters
goes. Pete chose this from mockups on 2026-09-29, pushed to his stack and answered there. Built the
same day in the working tree, not committed. "How it was built" lists the choices the plan left open.

The mockups were CSS, with the real fonts and mark colours. The generator is in the conversation's
scratchpad, not the repo. The renderer's spacing will differ slightly.

## Why

The outline makes notes look like meme captions. The outlines of neighbouring letters merge into
dark blobs, and every note reads as black weight. Boxes and arrows are plain strokes; notes were
the one mark that looked unlike the rest.

A solid tag reads on any background without an effect around the letters, so the text can be
smaller. Clean letterforms on a solid fill are also the easiest case for a model reading the
drawing after Send. No study compares note styles for vision models; the OCR studies found say
size after scaling and contrast matter most.

## Decisions

### The tag

- **Fill:** the mark's colour, red for a person and indigo for an agent
  (`docs/mark-colour-2026-09-29.md`). The words are white. A 1.5 pt white edge goes around the tag.
- **Corners:** one rule, a radius of half the height of a one-line tag. One line is a pill. More
  lines become a card with the same corners.
- **Size:** a person's note drops from 24 pt to 17 pt (`ui.newTextSize`), weight 560, line height
  1.32. Padding is 0.42 em above, 0.47 em below and 0.8 em at the sides, about 7, 8 and 14 pt at
  17 pt.
- **Width:** a note wraps at about 18 times its text size, 300 pt at 17 pt, or at the image's edge
  if that is closer. Lines are balanced: the tag takes the narrowest width that keeps the same
  number of lines, so no line ends with one word. The tag is as wide as its longest line.
- **Shadow:** a faint one, `0 0.5 1` at 22% black and `0 3 10` at 14%, cast by the white edge.
- **The outline goes.** `Mark.Text.outlineWidth` and `outlineColor` are removed, with the per-glyph
  stroke pass in `MarkRendering` and `EditorTextView`.

### A note on an arrow

- The note sits on the arrow's line, extended back past its tail. The tail touches the tag's
  rounded edge, so the arrow runs straight out of the note. This replaces the four sides in
  `notePlace(atTailOf:)` and the 8 pt `noteGap` for arrows.
- If the tag would leave the image, it moves inside, and the tail may then be off its edge.
- Pete looked at the other option, the tag on the arrow's midpoint, and chose the tail.
- Notes typed after a box keep `notePlace(beside:)` and its gap.

### An agent's notes

- **Colour:** indigo (`#364fc7`) for every agent, shapes and notes alike.
  `docs/mark-colour-2026-09-29.md` has the candidates. Claude's orange was tried: white on it is
  about 3 to 1, and orange sits near the person's red.
- **Font:** SF Mono, as now. Its size keeps today's rule, a fraction of the image's width
  (`agentTextSize` in settings.json), 1.33%, about 20 pt on a 3024 px capture at 2x.
- **The badge:** a white capsule on the tag's top edge, 0.35 em from its left, like a tab. It holds
  the agent's logo in its own colours and the agent's name ("Claude", "Codex"), in the style of the
  thumbnail's `AgentBadge`. It rises above the tag and overlaps its edge by 0.2 em, so it never
  covers the first line and the tag keeps a person's note's padding.

## What changes where

- **`MarkRendering.swift`** draws the tag, the text and the badge, for the PNG, cards, flights and
  the editor's bitmaps. It is the one drawer of a note's pixels, as now.
- **`EditorTextView.swift`** draws the note being typed the same way, so the tag is there while
  typing and nothing steps when typing ends.
- **`TextLayout` and `EditorGeometry`**: the wrap cap, balanced lines, the tag's box including its
  padding, and the placement at an arrow's tail. The tag's box is what a press hits, what
  selection outlines, and what `fittedSize` fits inside the image.
- **`ColorPass.swift`** picks the tag's colour. The measure moves from the letters against the
  pixels behind them to the tag's edge against the pixels around it. `docs/editor.md` and
  `docs/annotation-colour-2026-09-17.md` get the new measure.
- **The drawing format** gains an optional `agentName` on a mark (`"claude"`, `"codex"`), since a
  mark stores only `agent: true` today. It is filled from the card's agent when marks arrive with
  `add?agent=`, and from the request's client when they arrive in a reply. A mark without it shows
  a badge saying "Agent". See the open question on the format's version.
- **Docs:** `docs/editor.md` (the text outline row and the text size), `AGENTS.md` (the outline and
  the stroke pass in the renderer rule), and `docs/commands.md` if it states a text size.

Existing drawings open with their notes as tags at their stored size. There is no migration.

## Answers to the open questions

Pete accepted the recommendations on 2026-09-29.

1. **The format's version stays 1.** `Mark(validating:)` ignores a key it does not know, so a build
   without `agentName` reads the file and draws the note without the name.
2. **A note does not follow its arrow.** It is placed when it is typed.
3. **An agent's text is 0.0133 of the image's width.** Still to check on a wide stitch and a narrow
   capture.

Pete's answers on the follow-ups:

1. **Balance lines when typing ends, not while typing.** Built, as "Lines are balanced once typing
   ends" below says.
2. **Keep the shrink-then-wrap behaviour** near the right edge.
3. **Old drawings may shift** by a padding's width. Accepted.
4. **The agent colour.** Replaced by indigo in `docs/mark-colour-2026-09-29.md`.
5. **Long agent names and a lighter badge shadow.** Built, as the badge bullets below say.

## How it was built

- **A text's origin is the tag's top-left corner, and `wrap` is the widest the tag may be.** The
  words are inset by the padding. `TextLayout.box` is the tag, so hits, selection, resizing and
  `Mark.placed` work on the tag unchanged. A drawing saved before this opens with its notes a
  padding's width right of and below where the letters were.
- **Lines are balanced once typing ends.** While typing, a note's lines wrap where they reach the
  width, so words stay put under the caret (`TextStyle.balanced` is off for the text being typed).
  When typing ends, the text view stays over the note while its tag springs from the typed lines'
  rect to the balanced one and the words cross-fade (`TypingField.settle`, `EditorView.settleLines`).
  Both sets of words are clipped to the moving tag, and the selection outline and handles move with
  it. The note's bitmap stays hidden until the spring ends, then replaces the text view in the same
  commit. The time is `ui.noteSettleDuration`, 0.25 s, scaled by `motion`.
- **Shrinking while typing now stops once an 18 em tag fits the room.** A note near the right edge
  gets a little smaller and then wraps. Before, it shrank to half size before it wrapped.
- **An arrow's note** keeps the tail one stroke width inside the tag's edge, so the arrow's round
  cap is under the tag. `TextAnchor` has a `ray` for this. The room it grows into is the side the
  ray leads to.
- **The badge's width.** The layout sets the badge's own label (`NoteBadge`), so an agent's tag is
  at least as wide as its badge. A label past 10 em is cut with an ellipsis. `agent=` takes names
  up to 64 characters, and most are short.
- **The badge's shadow** is one soft shadow at 16% opacity, lighter than the tag's two. The badge's hairline
  edge already sets it off the tag, and a heavier shadow read as a smudge at the editor's size.
- **The logo** is rasterized once per agent, at 128 px, from its SVG (`AgentLogos`), so the
  renderer can draw it off the main thread. An agent without a logo gets the `cpu` symbol.
- **Settings defaults:** `newTextSize` 17, `textWeight` 600 (Semibold, the nearest system weight
  to the mockup's 560), `textLineHeight` 1.32.

## Verification

- Unit tests: `TextLayout` balancing and the width cap, the tail placement, and the tag's hit box,
  added to the existing editor geometry tests.
- The same screenshots as the mockups, drawn by the app: light, dark and busy, with notes of 1, 2
  and 5 lines, a note on an arrow, and an agent's note with its badge. Check them on a card, in
  flight, in the editor while typing and after, and in the PNG from Done.
- The typing-to-drawn handover, measured frame by frame as `docs/native-editor-2026-09-23.md` did.
  Nothing may step when typing ends.
- A long note's render time, against the 61 ms the outline pass took for 2,000 characters.

## What was verified (2026-09-29)

- **Unit tests:** all 411 pass. The geometry tests now cover the width cap, balanced lines, the
  tag's padding, and the note at an arrow's tail, including a diagonal arrow. The renderer test
  checks the tag's colour, its rounded corners, white words on red, and the agent's badge above
  the tag. The agent test checks that `agentName` survives a write and a read.
- **In the app:** a test copy on scratch settings showed person notes of 1, 2 and 5 lines on the
  light and dark mockup images, and a pushed Claude note with its badge and logo. They matched on
  the cards, in the editor, and in the PNG that Copy Drawing writes.
- **Typing:** an arrow drawn and a note typed at its tail. The frame while typing and the frame
  after Return differ only in letter-edge anti-aliasing, with no step. The text view puts its words
  at a whole-pixel inset, so the view is placed to match (`TypingField.inset`).
- **Render time:** a 2,000-character note draws in about 63 ms, against 61 ms with the outline.
  Its layout takes about 11 ms, most of it balancing.
- **The settle when typing ends,** recorded at 60 frames a second: the tag springs from three lines
  to its balanced three in about 0.2 s with no one-frame step. The words stay inside the tag, and
  the selection outline and handles move with it.
- **A long agent name** (`an-agent-with-a-very-long-name`) is cut with an ellipsis, each tag is at
  least as wide as its badge, and the badge's shadow reads as a faint edge in the editor.
