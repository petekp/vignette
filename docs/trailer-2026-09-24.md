# The hero trailer (2026-09-24)

The top of the landing page gets a short trailer. The detailed clips in
`docs/recordings-2026-09-24.md` move further down the page.

Pete watched the first cut of clip 3 and judged it too slow for a visitor's first seconds: "watching
someone draw a rectangle for almost 10 seconds and slowly type is just not going to fly."

## What the example has to do

Pete asked for "a masterful example of where Vignette is most useful". Vignette earns its place when
typing would take a paragraph and pointing takes a second. The example is judged on five tests:

1. **Words would fail.** Describing the problem takes a paragraph, and showing it takes a few boxes.
2. **The viewer has had this problem** in their own work.
3. **Claude's picture shows something you couldn't see yourself:** a state you'd have to go looking
   for, or something that doesn't exist yet. A picture of the fix fails, because a reload shows the
   same thing. It also has to be a decision that's yours, or Claude would just act.
4. **It reads with the sound off at page size.** The captions and the marks carry the story. The
   terminal can't, since its text is under 4 px on a phone.
5. **A real session would do every step,** given the prompt and the game's CLAUDE.md.

## Decided

- **A montage with captions** (2026-09-24). Each beat is cut on the action and has a short
  caption.
- **Vignette's own motion plays at its real speed.** Its speed is part of the pitch.
- **Cuts remove the waiting, and nothing is sped up.** The cuts skip the drawing, the typing and
  Claude's working time. The trailer never shows a faster app than the real one.
- **About 15 seconds, looping, at 1600 × 1000.**
- **You place the missing pieces of a bare scene** (2026-09-24). Level 4 starts without its moon,
  the city behind the roofs, or the water tower. You box where each one goes and label it, then
  Claude adds them. Placement is where words fail worst, and the scene filling in reads at any size.
  The gap and the moon bug were dropped because each can be typed in a few words.
- **Claude sketches the next step, and you adjust it** (2026-09-24). The game is about catching
  the moon, and the moon you placed is out of the cat's reach. Claude sketches a way up on a
  screenshot of the scene: three boxes for ledges, and "A way up to the moon. Move any ledge." You
  drag one box onto the water tower and Send it back, and Claude builds the ledges where the boxes
  are. The sketch shows something that doesn't exist yet, and where the ledges go is your call and
  about position. You edit Claude's marks like your own, which no other tool lets you do. A phone
  view with the moon cut off was tried first and dropped: Claude could have fixed it without
  asking, so the question looked like padding.
- **The history has its own beat** (2026-09-24). The fresh capture reaches the editor with a click
  on its thumbnail, since that is what anyone would do with a screenshot seconds old. The history
  comes in later, doing its real job: Claude's card slides away after five seconds, as every
  thumbnail does, and double-tapping right Shift brings it back.

## Smaller choices in the storyboard

Made while drawing it, each for a reason, and open to change:

- **The HUD is hidden.** The story doesn't need it, and the scene reads less busy without it.
- **The moon sits above the gap, left of centre.** A card's agent badge sits in its top-right
  corner (`StackView.swift`), and it would hide a moon there on Claude's card. The cat also looks
  up at it.
- **The labels are short,** "big moon", "a city behind" and "water tower", so they read at page size.
- **Claude's ledges are boxes with no arrows between them.** An arrow doesn't follow a box that
  moves (`docs/editor.md`, decision 3), so moving a ledge would leave an arrow pointing at nothing.
- **You move the middle ledge onto the water tower,** so the way up uses a piece you asked for.
- **Claude's text sits below the clouds,** because the captions cover the top-left corner.

## Needed before filming

- **The fix for the blank after Done.** The flight home is the payoff of frame 3, and today it
  starts with 8 to 9 frames of no picture (`docs/recordings-2026-09-24.md`, "First cut of clip 3").
  It changes app code, so it waits for Pete's approval.
- **herdr for the terminal.** Send reaches Claude Code only inside herdr, so the drop in frame 4
  lands in herdr too. A rehearsal there confirms Claude Code shows a dropped card as an image.
- **A clean set of sessions.** Send lists every Claude Code session in a herdr pane and every Codex
  thread, so a take on this Mac would show Pete's real work. The take needs a setup where only the
  Mew sessions exist.
- **Claude's setup.** A CLAUDE.md in the game's folder says the game is about catching the moon,
  and to sketch changes to a level's layout through Vignette before building them. The session allows the commands it needs, so it never
  stops on a permission prompt: rendering, `open vignette://add`, and reading Send's request folder.
- **Mew letterboxed at 16:10 in Safari,** so a drag around the game captures exactly the scene, with
  no empty bands above and below it.
- **The stack's other cards:** the sprite sheet, the title screen and the jump recording.

## Open, one at a time

1. **The caption wording,** whether the keys sit in the caption, and how big the captions are on a
   phone. The history's caption has to name right Shift, as the site does: ⇧⇧ alone doesn't say
   which Shift. At phone width the video is 358 px wide, and 34 px captions come out at about 8 px.
2. **How the drawing reaches Claude.** Done and then a drag works in any terminal, but the copy goes
   unused. Send from the editor would skip the copy and reach the same session in both directions,
   but it works for Claude Code only inside herdr.
3. **The ending.** The loop cuts from Claude starting on the ledges back to the capture.
4. **The stack's other cards,** which are placeholders in the storyboard.
5. **The terminal's theme.** A light terminal turns into a grey slab under the editor's dim. A dark
   one would sit back.
