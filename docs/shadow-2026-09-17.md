# One shadow from the card to the annotator and back (2026-09-17)

Pete: "thumbnail shadows still flicker once the annotator transitions back into a thumbnail; when a
thumbnail transitions to the annotator, the shadow under the annotator also flickers. Can't we keep
the same shadow between states but have different shadow property values for each state?"

This note says who draws the shadow in each state, what the camera found, and what changed.

## Who draws it

Three drawers, in three windows:

- the card in the stack: a SwiftUI `.shadow` inside `CardView`, in the stack panel (`.statusBar`);
- the flight: a SwiftUI `.shadow` on the flight image, in `TransitionLayer`'s panel (`.statusBar`,
  ordered in front of the stack panel);
- the annotator: `frameView`'s layer shadow, in the annotator window (`.floating`).

The flight already animates its shadow between the two ends: `TransitionLayer.Look` carries the
corner, the shadow's opacity, radius and y, and `fly` animates it from `.card(ui)` to
`.annotator(ui)` along the path. So the *values* already move continuously. What was wrong is
the moments at which one drawer takes over from another.

## What the camera found

Measured at 60 fps (`screencapture -v` over the region, every frame read back), on Pete's real `ui`
values, build `c43304a`.

**Handovers are atomic.** At every handover measured, one frame has the old drawer and the next has
the new one: never two shadows, never none. The stack panel and the flight panel change in the same
window-server frame, and so do the annotator window and the flight panel. The single-owner design
below is therefore not needed to stop a doubled or missing shadow; there was none to stop.

**The flight hands over before it has arrived.** `fly` called its completion on a timer,
`ui.expandDuration * 1.15` (0.46 s). A SwiftUI `spring(duration: 0.4, bounce: 0.15)` is still about
0.4 % from its target then. On the way in, that is 3 pt of the 1138 pt the card travels: the
flight's edge sat at column 234 of the capture for frames 90-94 and the annotator window's crisp
border appeared at column 240 on frame 95. The whole picture stepped 3 pt sideways in one frame,
and the shadow's edge with it. On the way back the same residual is 0.4 pt: the card's bottom
border moved up one pixel between frames 206 and 207.

**The card's shadow is weaker than the flight's, always.** The column is masked with a fade over
the panel's inset at each end (`StackView.column`), so a card leaving the viewport is not cut. The
padding that fade covers is the same 19 pt of room the shadows need, and the newest card rests
against the bottom of the viewport — so the bottom fade eats exactly the newest card's shadow,
while the flight, in another window, draws it in full. Lined up on the edge, the card's shadow read
0.98, 0.94, 0.89, 0.89, 0.81, 0.86, 0.80, 0.77 and 0.78 times the flight's at 1 to 8 pt below it.
When the card took over, the band 1-12 pt below it jumped 1.85 grey levels lighter and stayed
there. Confirmed by changing nothing but `ui.panelInset` from 19 to 60, which makes the same fade
four times gentler: the resting shadow then reads 0.8-1.4 grey levels *darker* at 1-6 pt below the
edge. A card in the middle of the column, whose shadow is not in the fade, lands with a step of
-0.7 instead.

So the flicker is not a shadow appearing twice or going missing. It is the picture and the shadow
stepping at the instant one drawer takes over from the other, because the flight has not finished
arriving and because the two drawers do not draw the same shadow at the same place.

## What changed

**Nothing takes a flight's place until it has arrived.** `fly` answers on `arrived` alone. A second
callback at the nominal `expandDuration * 1.15`, for anything the flight still covers at that point,
was written and then removed: the annotator window draws the same ring and shadow as the flight, at
the exact target, so putting it up while the spring is a few points short steps against the picture
the flight is still showing. Everything waits for `arrived`. The cost is the toolbar, which sits
below the frame and outside the flight image: it appears 0.14 s after the picture lands.
`arrived` runs when the spring is really there:
`Anim.settle` asks SwiftUI's own `Spring` when it will be within half a point — one Retina pixel —
of a target that far away, and the flight is put exactly on the target in that turn, so a card or a
window drawn there lands on the same pixels. The card retakes its slot on `arrived`, and
`TransitionLayer.lift(id:)` removes the flight image then, or later if the editor has not reported
`loaded` yet. For the screen-wide flight between the stack and the annotator that is 0.60 s instead of
0.46 s; the 0.14 s in between shows the flight image standing still where the card or the window
already is.

The flight's shadow is also cast by the clipped image, before the ring, the way a card casts its
own. Before, the flight's shadow came from the image *and* its half-point ring, which is a slightly
larger caster and a slightly heavier shadow than the card's.

**The column's fade starts below the newest card's shadow.** The bottom of the mask in
`StackView.column` is solid over the card area plus `StackLayout.cardShadowRoom` — how far a card
shadow reaches, `cardShadowY + cardShadowRadius * 2` — and fades over what is left of the inset.
With Pete's numbers that is 12 pt solid and a 7 pt fade. The same card now draws the same shadow
whether it is in the stack or in flight. The cost: a card scrolled out of the bottom of the column
fades over 7 pt instead of 19. The top fade is untouched, because a card shadow falls downwards and
never reaches into the top inset.

## The single-owner design, and why it is not built

Pete's phrasing suggests one shadow that exists from before lift-off until the card is back at
rest: the flight panel keeps a shadow-only element after its image is lifted, the annotator window
draws no shadow of its own, and the element follows the annotator's frame through every zoom step.
It is buildable: a rounded rect with a shadow and its interior punched out draws the same pixels
outside the frame as a shadow beneath it, and the flight panel is already above the annotator.

It is not built, for three reasons.

- It removes two handovers that measure clean. The camera found no doubled and no missing shadow at
  either end of the annotator.
- It does not remove the two that are left. The element still takes over from the card at lift-off
  and hands back at landing, so the card and the element must still draw the same shadow — the mask
  fix above is needed either way.
- The punch-out has to track the annotator's frame exactly, in another window, on every zoom tick.
  A fraction of a point of disagreement draws a dark line along the window's edge: a new artifact,
  in the place Pete is complaining about, to fix one that does not measure.

What Pete asked for is the behaviour, and the behaviour is now what he described: one shadow whose
values move between the card's and the annotator's, with nothing stepping when it changes hands.

## What the camera says afterwards

Same regions, same 60 fps, on the same `ui` values.

- **Into the annotator.** The left border used to sit at capture column 234 for five frames and
  then appear at column 240: three points in one frame. It now walks 231, 232, 233, 234, 235, 236,
  237, 238, 239, 240 — never more than one pixel a frame — and the band outside it settles without
  a step (15.50, 15.07, 13.32, 13.17, 13.15, 13.12, 13.03, 13.02, 13.02, and level from there).
- **Back to the card.** The band 1-12 pt below the newest card used to jump 1.86 grey levels in one
  frame and stay. Over six landings in a row it now decays smoothly — 18.8, 17.6, 16.7, 15.8, 15.2,
  14.7, 14.2, 14.1, 13.6, 13.3, 13.2, 13.0, 12.9, 12.7, 12.6 — with no frame changing by more than
  0.3 and no frame without a shadow.
- **The row under the card's edge** does not move when the flight lifts: 6.0/7.5/7.9/9.6/10.2/11.4
  before the lift and 5.8/7.4/8.0/9.2/10.2/11.4 after it.

## The other cases

- **A swap** (annotate a second card while the first is parking): two flights, each with its own
  `Look` and its own completion, guarded by the flight's generation, as before. A flight aimed
  somewhere else mid-air has its first completion dropped and the new one takes over.
- **A stitch converge**: the result does not move — it waits in the slot and fades up — so there is
  no arrival to wait for, and `converge` keeps its own timing. The pieces fade to nothing before
  they are removed.
- **Draw on New Captures**: the watcher's report re-aims the new card's flight at the annotator.
  That is a retarget, handled as a swap.
- **A lone thumbnail**: it leaves the panel when the annotator opens and comes back to the corner as
  an empty slot for the flight to land on. It is the bottom card of a one-card column, so it is the
  card the mask fix is about.
- **`ui.motion: 0` and Reduce Motion**: `expandDuration` is 0, so `Anim.settle` is 0 and both
  callbacks run in the turn after the flight is created — the stack appears and leaves without
  motion, as before.
- **A second display**: nothing here is in screen coordinates. The flight panel is placed on the
  screen the card is on and the mask is in the panel's own coordinates, so a display above the
  primary one, with negative y, changes nothing.
